## Scenario-Based Interview Questions – Payment & Order Failure Handling

Q1. What happens if payment succeeds but Order Service is down?
Answer: Payment success is persisted in the Payments DB. Order confirmation is retried asynchronously via a queue until the Order 
Service recovers.

Q2. Why is Payment Service the source of truth?
Answer: Because it handles money. Financial transactions must be strongly consistent and durable.

Q3. Why not make Order Service synchronous?
Answer: Because payment gateways are slow and unreliable. Blocking Order Service threads would reduce throughput and cause 
cascading failures.

Q4. How do you avoid duplicate order confirmations?
Answer: Using idempotency keys (orderId or paymentId) during order confirmation.

Q5. What is a Dead Letter Queue (DLQ)?
Answer: A queue that stores messages that failed processing after multiple retries, allowing inspection and replay without 
blocking the system.

Q6. Why is notification failure acceptable but payment failure is not?
Answer: Payment affects business correctness and money. Notifications only affect user experience.

Q7. How do retries work safely here?
Answer: Retries are safe because order confirmation is idempotent and retry attempts are limited with backoff.

Q8. What happens if Order Service recovers after 10 minutes?
Answer: It consumes pending events from the queue and confirms the orders without any user action.

Q9. Can notification be sent twice?
Answer: Yes, unless idempotency is applied at Notification Service. This is handled using (orderId, channel) tracking.

Q10. What consistency model does this system use?
Answer:
1) Strong consistency for payments and orders
1) Eventual consistency for notifications and side effects

Final Interview One-Liner: “We design payment systems to never lose money, even if that means temporary inconsistency elsewhere.”

## Interview Questions – Order Created, Payment Down

Q1. Why not make Order Service wait synchronously for payment?
Answer: Because payment gateways are slow and unreliable. Blocking threads reduces throughput and causes cascading failures.

Q2. How do you avoid double payment if user retries?
Answer: Using idempotency keys stored in DB and cache.

Q3. Why store idempotency keys in Redis?
Answer: For faster lookups during retries while keeping DB as source of truth.

Q4. What happens if Redis goes down?
Answer: Payment Service falls back to DB.

Q5. Why is payment consistency more important than order consistency?
Answer: Because payment involves money. Order state can be updated later.

Q6. What does the user see during payment timeout?
Answer: Order exists with PAYMENT_PENDING status.

Q7. How is final payment success detected?
Answer: Via webhook from payment gateway.

Q8. Why are webhooks necessary?
Answer: Payment gateways process asynchronously and cannot rely on synchronous responses.

Q9. What happens if webhook arrives twice?
Answer: Idempotency ensures safe handling.

Q10. What consistency model does this system use?
Answer:
1) Strong consistency for payments
2) Eventual consistency for order confirmation & notifications

Interview Closing Statement: “We design payment flows assuming failures, delays, and retries — correctness always wins over speed.”
